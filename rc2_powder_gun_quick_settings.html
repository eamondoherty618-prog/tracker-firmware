Quick Rules
  • Higher kV = better wrap on open flats, but more back-ionization risk.
  • Lower kV = better for second coats, candies, clear, and Faraday areas.
  • Lower µA helps the powder stop fighting you on recoats.
  • Only use as much PSI as needed to keep a clean powder cloud.
  • If the finish looks dry or dusty, first try lowering PSI and/or backing away a touch.
When to Change Settings Fast
  • Too much orange peel / back-ionization: drop kV and µA.
  • Not getting corners: drop kV, lower powder load, lighter passes.
  • Weak coverage on big flats: raise kV first, then fine-tune air.
  • Second coat won’t stick: go down hard on kV/µA.
  • Texture too light: raise film build and a little air, not just kV.
Source notes: RC-2 capability from Royal Coats RC-2 product page. kV / µA behavior and “high for open flats, low for corners and second coats” are consistent with Prismatic’s coverage-calculator guidance and general powder-gun guidance from Eastwood and Reliant.
